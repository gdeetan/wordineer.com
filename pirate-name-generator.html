    How to Use This Generator

    Pirate names work differently depending on what you need them for. A comedic pirate for a kids' book needs a name that's fun to say aloud — one that gets a smile, not a shudder. A menacing villain in an adventure novel needs something that implies a real body count. A legendary pirate for a tabletop campaign needs a name that sounds like it's been spoken around too many fires, passed down as warning. The three tone filters handle these different registers.

    The notoriety filter matters for scale. Scallywags get nicknames — "Scallywag Pete", "Fumble Jack", "One-Tooth Murray" — because they haven't earned a title. Captains have real authority over a ship; their names can carry titles. Admirals command fleets and represent institutional pirate power. Legendary pirates operate at myth scale — their name is more famous than they are, and half of what's said about them is probably false.

    Each result includes a lore tag — a short line that hints at a backstory element or reputation. These are starting points, not complete stories. "Known for sinking 12 merchant ships" is a reputation fact. "Identity unknown · leaves a playing card at every crime" is a mystery setup. Use whatever angle sparks something useful and discard the rest.

    What Makes a Good Pirate Name

    It should sound like a title, not just a name

    Real historical pirates had names that functioned as reputations. "Blackbeard" wasn't Edward Teach's birth name — it was a brand, communicating the terror he intended to inspire before anyone had to prove it. The best pirate names work this way: they announce something about the person before the person says a word. "Ironhand Mary" tells you she's been through something. "The Laughing Corsair" tells you she finds this funny.

    Descriptors beat invented names

    Pirate names that stick tend to be built from real descriptors — physical traits, methods, reputations — combined with a given name. "Saltbeard", "Ironkeel", "The Black Widow of the Sea" — these all describe something observable. Generic invented names (Zaxvorn the Dread) read as less pirate and more generic fantasy villain.

    The comedic register has its own rules

    Comic pirate names work through a specific kind of deflation — the name signals incompetence, bad luck, or unearned confidence. "Fumble Jack" implies a track record. "One-Tooth Murray" implies a story. "Scallywag Pete" implies he's been called that since before he deserved even the title scallywag. These names are funnier when they're specific rather than generic.

    Pirate Names for D&D and Tabletop

    Pirate campaigns — Ghosts of Saltmarsh, homebrew sea adventures, swashbuckling one-shots — need names at every level: the comedic barkeep who has a ship for sale, the menacing captain who runs the local sea lanes, the legendary admiral whose flag makes entire navies rethink their route. The notoriety filter maps directly to these levels of NPCs. For characters who sit at the villain end of the spectrum, the villain name generator handles names with darker organizational structures. For general fantasy character naming, the character name generator covers a wider range of settings and styles.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I use pirate names in my stories or games?
    Yes. Names aren't copyrightable, so any generated name is free to use in fiction, tabletop games, video games, or any creative project. Most generated names are original — just search a name if it seems familiar.
    What's the difference between scallywag and captain notoriety?
    Scallywags are low-level and often comic — nicknames rather than titles. Captains command ships and have real power. Admirals control fleets and are institutional threats. Legendary pirates have names that operate like myths — their deeds are so large they've become more story than person.
    What makes a pirate name sound authentic vs. silly?
    Authentic pirate names tend to be built from real descriptors combined with a given name. "Ironhand Mary" and "Saltbeard" work because they describe something. The comedic tone filter leans into silliness intentionally; the swashbuckler tone stays grounded in historical-feeling names.
    How do I build a complete pirate character from the name?
    Start with the lore tag — it's a short flavor hint that suggests a backstory angle. "Known for sinking 12 merchant ships" gives you a reputation. "Identity unknown · leaves a playing card at every crime" gives you a mystery. Use those hints as springboards, not scripts.
    Does this generator use AI?
    No. The generator uses curated name pools filtered by gender, notoriety, and tone. Every name and lore tag is handpicked — no language model generates them.
    Can I save pirate names?
    Yes — click the heart icon next to any name to save it to your Saved Names list below the results. Saved names persist as long as the tab is open. Copy them before closing.
