About words ending in -MENT

The "-ment" suffix entered English from French and Latin, where it formed nouns of action and result. Today it covers a wide range: "movement" names an ongoing action, "achievement" names a result, "instrument" names a tool, and "contentment" names a state. Because "-ment" words are often abstract, they appear heavily in academic, legal, and professional writing.

Part of speech: Nouns derived mainly from verbs. The suffix marks the action itself (movement), its result (achievement), its means (instrument), or its state (contentment).
