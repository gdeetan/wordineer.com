About words ending in -AGE

The "-age" suffix arrived in English from French and covers several distinct meanings. As a collective noun suffix it means "a collection of" (foliage, baggage). As a process noun it means "the act of" (storage, passage). As a place noun it marks a site or settlement (village, hermitage). This range makes "-age" words common across many registers — from casual (package, garbage) to formal (homage, heritage).

Part of speech: A mix of nouns denoting collection, place, or process (package, village, damage) and some verbs (manage, engage, ravage). The suffix entered English through Old French.

Using -AGE words in writing and word games

Short "-age" words score well in word games. "PAGE," "CAGE," "RAGE," and "SAGE" are all two-and-three-letter plays worth knowing for board extensions.
