    How to Use This Generator

    Hero names carry a different weight than ordinary character names. They need to signal something — a role, a capability, a mythology — without saying too much. Pick your genre first: fantasy names can be archaic and grand, superhero names tend to be punchy and conceptual, sci-fi names blend human familiarity with technological distance, and mythic names reach for the epic. Then use the Power Type and Tone filters to narrow the feel.

    The Power Type filter changes the kind of name you'll get more than you might expect. Strength names are solid and direct — they don't need decoration. Magic names are more ethereal, often with softer consonants and longer syllables. Stealth names are shorter and sharper, built for discretion. Intellect names suggest strategy and calculation. Elemental names invoke natural forces — they're wide rather than pointed. Mixing unexpected genre and power combinations often produces the most interesting results: a humble-tone intellect name for a fantasy setting, for instance, reads very differently than a grand mythic strength name.

    What Makes a Hero Name Work?

    The best hero names feel earned rather than assigned. They usually do one of three things: they describe the hero's most defining quality (Ironclad, Stormcall), they gesture at backstory without explaining it (The Paladin, Theron the Undying), or they work as titles the world has given them (Ajax Fireblood, Lysara Dawnbringer). Each approach creates a different relationship between the name and the character.

    Memorability

    A hero name gets spoken aloud a lot — by allies, by enemies, by narrators. If it trips off the tongue awkwardly or requires a pronunciation explanation every time, it becomes friction. Names with strong consonants and clean rhythm tend to stick. Say it aloud before committing: "And now, [name]!" If it lands cleanly, it works.

    Genre fit

    A superhero named Theron the Undying feels off-register. A fantasy knight named Axiom reads as anachronistic. Genre naming conventions exist because audiences have learned to read signals from names — they carry expectations. Honoring those signals doesn't mean following them blindly, but subverting them deliberately is different from ignoring them accidentally.

    Room for the character

    The name should leave space for who the character actually is. "Brightblade" tells you something about what they carry; it doesn't tell you who they are. That gap is where characterization lives. A name that over-explains the character can actually make them feel flatter, not more defined.

    Hero Name Conventions by Genre

    Fantasy

    Fantasy hero names often draw on invented phonetics (Arathor, Elyndra) or compound words with symbolic weight (Ironwill, Thornwall, Stormcall). Epithets are common — "the Bold", "the Undying", "Dawnbringer" — and they're usually titles the world has given, not chosen. If you want to explore the full range of fantasy character names beyond the heroic, the character name generator covers realistic, horror, historical, and sci-fi settings too. And if you need a name for a specifically magical character — elves, sorcerers, fae — the fantasy name generator focuses on invented-world name construction.

    Superhero

    Superhero names are almost always one word or a two-word compound. They name the power (Tempest, Ironclad), the role (Bulwark, Aegis), or the method (Shadowbane, Veilstrike). They're designed to be recognizable at a shout — in an action sequence, you don't have time for complexity. Avoid proper names for superhero identities unless there's a specific reason; they tend to dilute the mythic register.

    Sci-Fi

    Sci-fi heroes are often given real-sounding names (Commander Vance, Navigator Ryn) that feel grounded in a futuristic military or institutional structure. Alternatively, they're referred to by designation (Spectre-7, Centurion-7, Protocol ARES) which implies a dehumanizing system they're working against. Both approaches carry tone — choose deliberately.

    Mythic

    Mythic names reach for the ancient and the earned. They're often structured as "Name the Epithet" (Theron the Undying, Ajax Fireblood) or they simply carry enough syllable weight to feel legendary (Kronus, Aethon of the Flame). If you're naming a villain for this hero to face down, the villain name generator is built on the same structure and generates names that pair well with heroic counterparts.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I use these names in my published work?
    Yes. Names aren't copyrightable, so you can use any generated name in fiction, games, scripts, or published work. If a name is very close to a famous existing character, do a quick search to avoid confusion — but for most original-sounding names, there's no legal concern.
    What makes a good hero name vs a bad one?
    A good hero name is memorable when spoken aloud, fits the genre's conventions, and hints at character without spelling everything out. Bad hero names are either too generic (The Protector) or too on-the-nose (Mighty Justice Man). The best ones feel earned — like a title the character grew into.
    What does the Power Type filter affect?
    Power Type filters the name pool to match a specific heroic archetype. Strength names tend to be solid and grounded. Magic names are more ethereal. Stealth names are shorter and sharper. Intellect names suggest strategy. Elemental names invoke natural forces. Mixing genres and power types often produces the most interesting combinations.
    How is this different from the character name generator?
    The character name generator focuses on realistic-feeling names for any character across genres. This generator is specifically tuned for heroic names — names that carry weight, imply a role, and feel like they belong to someone who matters in the story.
    Does this generator use AI?
    No. The generator uses curated name pools filtered by genre, power type, and tone. Every name is handpicked — no language model is involved. That means you won't get unpronounceable nonsense or names that don't match the genre you selected.
    Can I save hero names?
    Yes — click the heart icon next to any name to save it to your Saved Names list below the results. Saved names stay as long as the tab is open. Copy them before closing the page.
