What Is an SAT Words List?

The SAT words list includes over 400 words found in academic register, or serious nonfiction, used on the new digital SAT© for Reading and Writing tests. This SAT words list includes word definitions, sample sentences, syllable count, root language, and difficulty ratings for each word.

This page is designed to accompany our interactive SAT Vocabulary Words tool, which can generate a static list of words as well. The main difference is that on this page you can read through the entire SAT vocabulary base, and then go to the tool to practice one word at a time in a variety of modes including audio. You can also print out this page to study with.

What does it mean for a word to be SAT-level? In the past, SAT vocabulary was limited to a small dictionary of somewhat obscure words that were primarily tested through definition. Today, in the post-2016 SAT, most of the vocabulary appears in context, but having a strong command of vocabulary throughout the test, especially in Words in Context items, which test students' ability to understand near-synonyms, is key. Most of the words on this list are tenuous, candid, or something you can substantiate. They are far more typical of the modern test than Suns, GRNs, FYEs, or GOBs.

How to Use This List to Study

Using a reference list for SAT vocabulary is very different from using flashcards. The goal of a reference list for studying vocabulary is to understand the words in layers, often by going through the list in passes over time. The following is a commonly effective way to go through a reference list of SAT vocabulary words:

  • First pass — Easy tier. Refer to the word difficulty filter to show only words that have been determined to be "Easy" for you. You read through these words in a session, but in general, you only need to read through these words for recognition purposes (you will have a sense of the general meaning of these words after reading through this list for recognition purposes).
  • Second pass — Medium tier. Filter your list for medium difficulty words after you have gone through the Easy words a few times. For each of these words, read the definition first, followed by the sample sentence. The sample sentence will illustrate connotation and register, important concepts for understanding word meaning that the definition does not fully capture.
  • Third pass — by root language: use the Root filter for Latin words, then for Greek words, and finally for French words. This is because many related words fall into each family, and studying their etymology will also help you recognize many other words. In this way, you will be able to recognize many words on test day, as long as you recognize the root of the word.
  • Final pass — Review hard words. Filter to hard and read through the vocabulary slowly in this final session. The Hard tier vocabulary will highlight the difference between a strong score and a top score on the SAT Reading and Writing sections.
  • Print a subset of words. You can choose to print a list of words based on any of the filters that you have chosen (e.g., the list of words that are comprised of only Latin roots, all of the words with negative connotations, etc.). The resulting list will be a clean handout that you can take with you and use for other studying or for marking up in paper copies of the Vocabulary Tool.

After reading through the above list of SAT vocabulary words, move on to the corresponding tool page, SAT Vocabulary Words. Here you can practice recalling each word one at a time in Practice Mode, where the definition is hidden from view. This page is meant to be used in conjunction with the above list page.

Most Important SAT Root Words

There is one single highest-leverage activity in SAT vocabulary preparation: learning the Latin and Greek roots. One root can yield dozens of word definitions, and typically it is the root of an unfamiliar word that allows you to get to the correct answer by understanding the context in which the word is used. Below are some high-yield roots, with examples of words that use each root, filtered below.

  • ben- (good): benefit, benevolent, benefactor, benign, beneficiary. Recognize ben- at the start of a word, and you usually have a positive connotation.
  • mal- (bad): malicious, malevolent, malign, malefactor, malady. The mirror image of ben- — a word that starts with mal- almost always carries harm or wrongdoing.
  • -dict- (to say): predict, contradict, edict, malediction, indictment, dictum. One of the most productive roots in academic English.
  • -loqu- / -loc- (to speak): eloquent, loquacious, colloquial, soliloquy, grandiloquent. Speech-related vocabulary clusters densely here.
  • -gen- (birth, origin): generate, genesis, indigenous, progeny, congenital. Often appears in science and biology passages.
  • -chron- (time): synchronize, anachronism, chronic, chronology. Frequently appears in history and literary criticism.
  • -phil- (love of): philosophy, philanthropy, bibliophile, philology. Greek-root cluster, common in humanities passages.
  • -vert- / -vers- (to turn): divert, avert, subvert, invert, introvert, conversion. A Latin workhorse.
  • -ped- (foot or child): pedantic, expedite, impede, pedestrian, pediatric. Watch context — the same root carries two meanings.
  • -script- / -scrib- (to write): prescribe, inscription, manuscript, conscription, transcribe. High-frequency in the Writing section.

Use the Root filter above to get a list of just the Latin or Greek words. Study them as families. One session per major root will generally get more out of your time studying them as related words than scattered attempts to memorize a number of unrelated words.

SAT Words With Definitions vs SAT Vocabulary Practice

There are essentially two ways to study SAT vocabulary, and you should use both. First, reading through a list of words with their definitions included can be very good for familiarizing oneself with the vocabulary of the test. That is what this page is for. Another way to study vocabulary is through active recall, or testing yourself to recall a word's meaning from memory. That is how the SAT Vocabulary Words tool can be used, especially in its Practice Mode.

This is how I approach vocabulary in a sequence: 1) start with this list of words and study them by reading through the list of words and their definitions to get familiar with them; 2) filter out the words you don't know yet; 3) skim through the list of words you know to get them down to memory; 4) print out the words you don't know yet and review them; 5) use the SAT Vocabulary Words tool to generate a list of 10-15 words at a time, practice their recall in Practice Mode, and save the words you get wrong to your saved list of words, reviewing them in a few days. This will give you a broad base of vocabulary and then precisely drill the vocabulary you need to improve. Study the words in this order, and they will stick.

Frequently asked questions

How many words are on the SAT list?
Note that the College Board no longer publishes an official SAT word list after the 2016 redesign of the test. The following list includes 400+ words that frequently appear in SAT Reading passages as well as in Writing tests. These words are found in the academic register that the SAT tests.
Should I memorize every word on the list?
No. I would say you should focus on the 'easy' and 'medium' level words first to build a solid base. Then, use the above filter to focus on the hardest words in the list. 200-300 familiar words are far more valuable than spending a few minutes familiarizing yourself with the full list of 400+ words.
How is this different from the SAT Vocabulary Words tool?
This single page displays all the words for browsing, reading, searching, and printing. In contrast, the SAT Vocabulary Words tool creates random study lists and includes audio, saved lists, and a single-word practice mode. Use this page for general review and the SAT vocabulary tool for drilling.
Can I print this list?
Yes. This printable SAT study list can be created by clicking on the "Print List" link at the bottom of this page, or by using your browser's print functionality (e.g., "Ctrl+P" on Windows or "Cmd+P" on Mac). Note that the print view automatically hides the tools used to navigate and to filter the list of SAT vocabulary words, so that all you are left with is a clean printout of your study list, including definitions and example sentences.
What does the root note tell me?
For each word, the 'Root Note' provides etymology for words (Latin, Greek, French, etc. origin and notes on how the word entered the English language). This helps test-takers recognize many unfamiliar words when they have the same prefix and root as other words on the test.
Does the SAT still test vocabulary directly?
These are not individual vocabulary questions. All of the vocabulary is tested in context in the many Words in Context questions throughout the Reading and Writing sections of the test. This means students must have knowledge of precise word meanings and of their connotations. Thus, example sentences, used here to help explain a word's meaning, are crucial for effective test preparation.
