About words ending in -IVE

The "-ive" suffix marks adjectives of tendency or nature. "Active" means tending to act, "creative" means tending to create, "effective" means producing effects. The same suffix sometimes tips into noun territory: a "detective" is one who detects, a "native" is one born to a place. The dual use is worth knowing for reading comprehension across formal texts.

Part of speech: Mostly adjectives meaning "tending to," "having the nature of," or "performing" some action (active, creative, massive). Some are nouns (motive, native, detective).

Using -IVE words in writing and word games

"GIVE," "LIVE," "DRIVE" — many common English verbs end in "-ive" without the suffix, but the true "-ive" adjectives still give you useful word-game options in tight positions.
