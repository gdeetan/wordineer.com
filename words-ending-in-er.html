About words ending in -ER

The "-er" suffix does two very different jobs. As an agent suffix it converts verbs into people or tools: "write" becomes "writer," "run" becomes "runner," "compute" becomes "computer." As a comparative suffix it modifies adjectives to mean "more": "fast" becomes "faster," "bright" becomes "brighter." Both uses are highly productive and appear constantly in both speech and writing.

Part of speech: Two distinct groups: agent nouns (someone who performs an action — teacher, runner) and comparative adjectives (bigger, faster). A third smaller group includes nouns with no obvious suffix origin (answer, number, corner).

Using -ER words in writing and word games

Agent nouns ending in "-er" are among the easiest words to score in word games because the base verb is already common. If you know a short verb that fits the board, check whether its "-er" form is also valid.
