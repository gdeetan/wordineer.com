    What is a Never Have I Ever Generator?

    This tool will randomly create a list of Never Have I Ever statements that you can use for your game straight away. No more stalling for your game when all of your questions have been asked. Simply choose a category for your statements, enter how many you would like to create, and click “Generate.” New statements will be created ready for the next round of your game before you have even finished reading the previous ones.

    The generator creates statements in 7 categories: Funny, Kids, Teens, Adults, Drinking, Couples, and Deep. Each of these categories is for different groups of people and for different occasions. All of the statements were written to be as real as possible and thus to differ from generic versions of the game. For example, “Never have I ever added something I already completed to my to-do list just so I could tick it off” is so much funnier than “Never have I ever been embarrassed”. The generator is built to create very specific statements.

    Why Use a Never Have I Ever Generator?

    The primary purpose of using a generator to create statements is to provide convenience to the game player. There are many, many phrases available for use, and a generator ensures that you won’t “run out” of good statements in the middle of the game. More importantly, however, a generator takes a huge load of social pressure off of the individual determining what the next statement should be. The vast majority of individuals will choose to go with the “safe” and “obvious” option for a given situation when they are pressured to come up with a response in real time.

    Using a curated question bank, rather than random statements, will make your rounds of conversation feel fresh and lead to more genuine reactions than cycling through the same 30 or so questions most people use when playing without aid. After some time playing without a tool to aid your questions, you will likely come to realize that the goal of the game is not to find out who has done the most things, but to discover specifically which things they have done, and get to know the members of your group, in all of their delightful variation, better.

    Different categories will be more suitable for professional and educational settings. For example, the “Funny” category only contains statements that provide some humor for a workplace and is therefore suitable for mixed age groups. The “Deep” category is more suitable for small groups of close friends who want to have a more serious conversation than in a party-like setting.

    How to Play Never Have I Ever

    The core mechanics are simple. One player reads a statement aloud, beginning with "Never have I ever..." Everyone who has done the thing described reacts — the reaction depends on which version you're playing:

    • Fingers down: Everyone starts with 10 fingers up. Each time you've done the thing stated, you put one finger down. The first player to have no fingers left loses — or wins, depending on how you look at it.
    • Points version: Start with 10 points. Lose one point for each statement you've done. The last player with points remaining wins.
    • Drinking version: Anyone who has done the thing takes a sip. No elimination — just continuous play until you've gone through all the statements.
    • Show of hands: Everyone simply raises a hand if they've done it. No tracking, no losing — best for group discussions and icebreakers where the goal is conversation rather than competition.

    Generate a round of statements before the game starts, or hit Generate after each round ends to keep the pace moving. The tool tracks every statement it has shown in the current session, so you won't see repeats until you've gone through the full category.

    How the Generator Works

    Select a category from the dropdown menu. Enter the number of statements you wish to have generated for the round (1–20). Click the Generate Statements button to have the tool select and present a random set of statements from the questions for the selected category.

    Clicking “hide cards” blurs all statements and has one person reveal them one at a time. Clicking “save” saves favorite cards to a shortlist from round to round. If you click on “copy all,” you get a plain text version, and the print option shows a clean, ad-free layout.

    Categories — What Each One Contains

    Funny: These are little moments of awkwardness that everyone can relate to. Such as: waving at someone who was waving at the person behind you, replying to a waiter with “you too,” or pressing the button for a lift that is already lit. The Funny category contains all of these situations and is the best choice to start with if you are unsure about the audience in front of you.

    Kids: This game is based on childhood experiences and misdeeds. It is 100% family-friendly, and I recommend it for kids aged 6 and up.

    Teens: A special edition for social media-savvy teenagers dealing with school life. Includes topics like accidentally liking an old photo, canceling plans at the last minute, and pretending to listen to someone. For 13–17-year-olds.

    Adults: Adults experiencing the peculiar variety of embarrassment that stems from work-related situations (misunderstandings in emails, forgotten subscriptions, pretending to be paying attention in meetings, etc.). Safe for mixed groups of professionals.

    Drinking: For the occasion when “Kids or Teens” are involved with drinks. Each statement is about nights out and the corresponding experiences and decisions that have been made in those situations. For adults only.

    Couples: Contains various statements about the usual habits of couples and their honest confessions. As with “Drinking,” it is best played as part of a larger social occasion with plenty of wine. But here it is particularly well-suited for a fun date night rather than a standard board game session.

    Deep: These are deep statements about regret, honesty, loneliness, and quiet pride. Good for a small group of close friends having a meaningful game night instead of just a party game.

    How to Run a Great Round

    There are simple mechanics behind the session, but there are a number of variables that need to be managed to ensure a good-quality session.

    • Match the category to the room. A Funny session is always safe; a Drinking session is for adults but assumes they are comfortable with the discussion. A Deep session is best suited for small, close-knit groups and should not be started with Deep prompts for people who have just met.
    • Encourage follow-up conversations. The most meaningful moments in the game occur when someone reacts to a prompt, and then you can ask them for more information about it (e.g., “You put a finger down — what happened?”). The generator creates the prompts for you, but the conversations are yours to have.
    • Use the ‘Save’ button. Save statements that lead to great stories or great reactions, and they will be added to your personal shortlist of the best prompts for your group of friends.
    • Use Hide Cards for slow reveal. When playing in a group setting, blur all the cards first, then have one person reveal a statement at a time while the other players wait their turn to put a finger down.
    • Set how many statements you want generated for your group size (5 for 4–6 people, 8–10 for 7+ people). When you have gone around the circle, generate the next round of statements.

    Never Have I Ever as a Work Icebreaker

    This category of funny, adult statements for professional settings is safe to use in workplaces without the risk of exposure that personal games like Never Have I Ever carry. “Never have I ever nodded along in a meeting when I had absolutely no idea what was happening” gets universal reactions because everyone in any work environment has done this at some point. They are embarrassing enough to get a genuine reaction but not so personal as to be awkward.

    The show-of-hands version is the best way to experience “Never Have I Ever… at work” with your team or department. There’s no need for drinking or elimination here. People just raise their hands in response to each question and can then tell a story about their answer. These are great to use as a pre-workshop activity, to fill time between the end of one meeting session and the beginning of another, or as a way to start off an all-hands meeting and get people talking.

    Never Have I Ever FAQs

    How many statements should I generate per round?
    For most groups, 5 phrases are the default, enough for 4 to 6 people to go around in a circle without dragging on too long. For larger groups or longer sessions, you can generate 8 to 10 Statements. For the show-of-hands icebreaker version of the game, 5 to 7 phrases are usually enough to keep people interested before they start to wander off.
    What category should I start with?
    You could start off playing the Funny statements for the initial icebreaker, as they are the most Relatable. Once you get into the statements and everyone starts to get into the game, you can then switch to the Adults or the Couples statements. The Deep statements are usually reserved for the smallest number of people (fewer than 6) where everyone knows each other very well and is very comfortable with each other.
    Can I use this as a drinking game?
    Yes, by choosing the “Drinking” category. This category consists of stories about nights out and social situations of adults. For each statement, read it aloud, and anyone who has done it takes a sip of their drink. This category is for adults and should be played in a setting where you would have a normal conversation with friends. The simpler the format of the game (i.e., no extra rules), the better it is.
    Is there a version suitable for kids?
    All of the statements in the Kids category are of a variety of childhood situations and are quite embarrassing but NOT objectionable in any way. So, kids as young as 6 can play it, and it's family-safe. And, of course, the kids' versions can be played with fingers down instead of drinking, making it more competitive for youngsters.
    What happens when I run out of statements?
    For each round of statements, the generator shows all unshown statements for the current session from the chosen category, before cycling back to the start of the category’s pool of statements once all statements have been shown. The generator can be reset at any time to clear the tracking for the current session, without changing any of the other settings.
    Can I use this as an icebreaker at work?
    All statements for Funny and Adults work in a professional setting. Funny statements relate to familiar situations like nodding in a meeting you don’t understand, instantly forgetting a person’s name after having introduced yourself to them, or pressing a button for an already operating lift. Workplace statements are played out with a show-of-hands variation, so that participants can respond if they wish to, but are under no pressure to do so.
    What's the difference between Adults and Deep?
    Adults contains statements dealing with the very special kind of social awkwardness that comes with adult life. Work emails, long-forgotten subscriptions, lying to your spouse about being on the way home – yes, that’s the kind of stuff. Deep is another story altogether. It’s more emotional and truthful than Funny, focusing on things like regret, loneliness, things left unsaid. Note that Deep statements are best for very small groups of people who know and trust each other. It doesn’t make for a very good icebreaker.

    Who Uses the Never Have I Ever Generator?

    Party hosts
    Generate a full round before guests arrive so you're not scrambling for prompts mid-game. Use the Funny or Drinking categories for large groups and save the best statements for a curated shortlist you can reuse.
    Families with kids
    The Kids category gives you 20 age-appropriate statements that get genuine reactions from children — sneaking snacks, blaming siblings, staying up past bedtime. The fingers-down version is a great car journey or dinner table game.
    Couples
    The Couples category surfaces honest, specific confessions about relationship habits — things that are true for almost every couple but rarely get said out loud. Use it on a date night or whenever you want a real conversation without it feeling forced.
    Team leads and facilitators
    The Funny category works reliably as a workplace icebreaker — show-of-hands format, no personal risk, high relatability. It takes under five minutes and produces real reactions from people who might not usually engage with icebreaker formats.
