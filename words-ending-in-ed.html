About words ending in -ED

The "-ed" suffix is the default past-tense marker in English. Regular verbs simply append it (walk → walked, play → played). The result doubles as a participial adjective, which is why the same word can appear in "she finished the race" (verb) and "a finished product" (adjective). Understanding this overlap helps readers and writers interpret "-ed" words correctly in context.

Part of speech: Most are verbs in past tense or past participle form. Many also serve as adjectives (tired, excited, abandoned) when placed before a noun or after a linking verb.

Using -ED words in writing and word games

Past-participle adjectives ending in "-ed" are useful in writing because they pack a lot of meaning into a single modifier. "An exhausted runner" says more than "a runner who is tired."
