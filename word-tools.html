    How word generators help writers

    The word generator allows you to select words from a list of English words. You can filter by word type (such as noun, verb, adjective, adverb), by level of difficulty, and by starting letter. Novelists use this tool to come up with a character's odd little habit, or the one object sitting on a table that the reader will remember.

    Poets look for nouns that have a slightly wrong connotation. Screenwriters use it to try to come up with alternatives to the obvious first way to develop a scene.

    Instead, generate five random sentences and start your scene with the most awful of them. This forces you to think about your scene in a very different way, usually for the better. And for building vocabulary of word forms, such as past tenses, comparative words, etc., the Word Scramble and Word Unscramble tools enable students to practice spelling in a way that closely simulates how words enter the mind and are stored for later use.

    Building vocabulary without the flashcard slog

    I mentioned earlier that most people give up on their flashcards by the second week. There are much better, less time-consuming ways to build your vocabulary. For one, you can use the random word tool to see a single word and then immediately read the definition and a sample sentence of how it is used. For example, right now I could see a word, decide in a second whether I know it, and then read the definition above.

    Many ESL learners use this tool daily to study for school. Many writers use it to come up with lists of synonyms for words they've forgotten that they know.

    A Word of the Day can also be placed on your computer as a Desktop Shortcut to be opened every morning with your coffee, and within a month you will have learned thirty words that you thought you never could learn, for words that did not feel like studying.

    This is not a replacement for a dictionary or your grammar texts, but it is a good first stop when you need a quick word. However, I can make it easier for you to get to the words you need. Thirty seconds or so per word. Try these tools and see what works for you.

    Tools for teachers and students

    Each tool works on any device with a browser, making them perfect for in-class use. You can project the random word generator and have students try to come up with a definition for a given word within a two-minute time frame. Then you can reveal the correct answer. The sentence generator gives you a new writing prompt each time you hit reload, which makes it a great worksheet filler.

    For timed writing sections such as those found on the SAT or ACT, write one sentence first. Generate a sentence in advance using Word of the Day. Write one sentence that expresses your meaning using the word correctly. Then, start your timed essay in 30 seconds. This helps to avoid the biggest problem of the first part of the test: choosing a topic.

    All of these tools are also great for classroom lessons, like dictation practice or word scrambles. You can print out the scrambled words and have your students try to spell them as fast as possible. And if you're looking for a classroom game, the Pictionary word generator is a good choice. The teacher tools hub lists the tools that teachers have actually bookmarked.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a random word generator?

    The random word generator selects a random word from a list, with filters to narrow results by type, difficulty, and length. The list can be further limited by type (nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs), difficulty, first letter, and word length. Writers, students, and teachers all use the random word generator for creative writing prompts and activities. Students can use the tool to practice vocabulary. Teachers can also use Wordineer for a quick classroom activity. Each word includes the definition so you always know what it means.

    Which tool should I use for creative writing?

    It depends on whether you need a starting word or a starting sentence. For starters, the random word generator can provide you with a seed for your writing. The random sentence generator can provide you with a scaffold for your writing. A word can be a name, a detail, or concept that you can start to build around. A sentence can be something for you to react to, or the start of a larger piece of writing.

    Are these word tools free?

    Yes. The entire site is free to use, with no word count or time limits. The site is funded by ads displayed on each page. There is no need to register for an account and all of the tools work instantly and without installation of any software.

    How do teachers use these tools in the classroom?

    For warm-ups, teachers usually have students first define words they've read in context, then move on to the lesson. For spelling weeks, teachers often use the scramble tool to make a paper-based race for their students to unscramble words. The word tools can be used right in a browser, and run on old Chromebooks and even on students' phones. There are no accounts to set up, and no software to download.

    What is the difference between a word generator and a sentence generator?

    The Word Generator creates one word every time you press Generate, while the Sentence Generator creates one sentence every time you press Generate. Both generators serve as quick starting points for writing or reacting. Use them when you need a concept, a noun, a few words to start off a short piece of writing, etc.

    What is word scramble and how is it different from word unscramble?

    Word scramble games contain the same letters, and these are mixed up so that you have to find the correct word (like a good spelling game). A word unscramble program contains a set of letters and searches for all words that can be created with these letters. This makes it a useful tool for word games like Scrabble and Wordle.
