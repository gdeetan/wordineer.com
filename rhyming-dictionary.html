How to use the rhyming dictionary

Type any word into the search box and the dictionary instantly shows all matching rhymes from our database of 1,300+ words. Results are grouped into 1-syllable, 2-syllable, and 3+ syllable categories so you can quickly find the perfect fit for your poem or song.

Use the part of speech filter to narrow results to nouns, verbs, adjectives, or adverbs. Click any rhyme word to instantly search for its own rhymes — great for building rhyme chains. Hit Random to explore words you might not have thought of.

Tips for better rhymes

  • Match syllable count to keep your rhythm consistent.
  • 2-syllable rhymes often sound more natural in verse than single-syllable ones.
  • If you can't find a perfect rhyme, try a near rhyme (same vowel sound, different ending).
  • Use "Copy all rhymes" to paste a full list into your lyrics or poem draft.

Frequently asked questions

What is a rhyming dictionary?

A rhyming dictionary finds words that share the same ending sound as a given word. Unlike a regular dictionary (which defines words), a rhyming dictionary is organized by sound — making it an essential tool for poets, lyricists, rappers, and anyone who writes in verse.

How many words are in this rhyming dictionary?

Our dictionary covers over 1,300 common English words organized into 40+ phonetic rhyme groups. We focus on the words most useful for everyday writing rather than obscure terms, so you're always getting practical, usable rhymes.

What's the difference between a perfect rhyme and a near rhyme?

A perfect rhyme shares the exact same vowel sound and ending consonant (cat/hat, light/night). A near rhyme (also called a slant rhyme or half rhyme) shares a similar but not identical sound (cat/bad, light/like). Near rhymes are widely used in modern poetry and songwriting for a less sing-songy feel.

Why does syllable count matter when choosing a rhyme?

Syllable count affects the rhythm (meter) of a line. In structured poetry forms like sonnets or haiku, matching syllables is essential. Even in free verse or song lyrics, pairing words of the same syllable length keeps your lines sounding balanced and natural.

Can I use this for rap lyrics?

Absolutely. Rap relies heavily on multi-syllable rhymes and rhyme chains. Use the syllable filter to find 2- and 3-syllable matches for richer rhymes, and click any result word to chain into its own rhyme set — a technique used by many professional lyricists to keep rhyme schemes flowing.
