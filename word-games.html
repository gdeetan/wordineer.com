About these games

Party games and word games are two different things. Party games — Would You Rather, Truth or Dare, Catchphrase — are social by design. Someone holds the phone and controls the flow. Everyone else reacts, argues, or gets embarrassed. The person with the phone doesn't need to set anything up; they just tap. Word games like Hangman and word searches can work the same way, but they're also fine to play alone on a commute.

Would You Rather and Truth or Dare actually work better on a phone than on a dedicated app. You don't need everyone to see the screen at once — the person holding it reads the question out loud and the group goes from there. Same with Catchphrase: one person sees the word, everyone else tries to guess it from clues. The generator handles the pacing; you handle the shouting.

Charades and Pictionary have a different constraint: the person drawing or acting must not see the word before everyone else. Both generators here reveal words on demand, one at a time, so you can hand the phone to whoever's up next without the whole group seeing what's coming. Scattergories and word searches sit somewhere in between — they're built for groups but work fine solo, and teachers use them regularly for vocabulary work in classrooms and ESL contexts.

Common questions

Do these games require an internet connection after the page loads?

No. All the game logic and word lists are bundled into the page itself. Once it loads, you can put your phone in airplane mode and keep playing. The initial load requires a connection, but that's it.

How many people do you need for these games?

It depends on the game. Would You Rather, Truth or Dare, and Catchphrase work with two people and get better with more. Charades and Pictionary need at least three — one person to act or draw, and others to guess. Scattergories is flexible: two people competing is fine, and it scales well to larger groups. The word search and Mad Libs tools work solo.

Can I use these with kids?

Yes — most tools have an audience or difficulty filter. Truth or Dare has a kids mode with tame prompts. Charades lets you filter by category (animals, movies, etc.) to keep it age-appropriate. Would You Rather has a difficulty setting you can keep at "mild" for younger players. Scattergories and word searches are fine for any age.

Are there categories I can filter by?

Several of the tools support category filtering. Charades lets you pick from categories like animals, movies, celebrities, and actions. Pictionary words are sorted by difficulty tier. Truth or Dare lets you filter by audience type. Scattergories generates full category lists with a random letter. Check the individual tool for its specific options.
