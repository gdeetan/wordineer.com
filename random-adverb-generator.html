    What is a Random Adverb Generator?

    The Random Adverb Generator pulls random English adverbs from a hand-curated list, optionally accompanied by a definition. Unlike the Random Word Generator, which generates random words regardless of their grammatical function, this tool is specifically targeted at the class of words that end in -ly. It has 6 filters for different types of adverbs, different levels of difficulty, and adverbs that start with the first or last letter of the word. It also has a definitions toggle.

    The 5 Types of Adverbs Explained

    English adverbs are split into five functional categories. Understanding them makes both the filters and your own writing more precise.

    • Manner — Manner adverbs describe how an action is performed. Some examples include gracefully and boldly. They answer the question "how?" and are the most common adverb type in fiction and everyday prose. "She moved gracefully through the crowd" tells the reader far more than "she moved through the crowd."
    • Time — Time adverbs say when something happens. Some examples include immediately and recently. They anchor events on a timeline and control the pacing of the narrative. "He had recently returned from the coast" creates backstory in five words; "he returned immediately" drives urgency.
    • Place — Place adverbs indicate where an action occurs. Some examples include nearby and elsewhere. They orient the reader spatially without requiring a full prepositional phrase. "She looked elsewhere" is tighter than "she looked in another direction."
    • Degree — Degree adverbs modify adjectives, verbs, or other adverbs to indicate intensity or extent. Examples: extremely, barely. They are among the most powerful tools for precision: "barely audible" and "extremely audible" describe opposite ends of the same spectrum using one word each.
    • Frequency — Frequency adverbs express how often something happens. Examples: rarely, always. They establish patterns and habits in prose and conversation. "She rarely spoke in meetings" tells the reader something about character without a longer character description.

    The adverb type filter maps directly to these five categories. Select one to narrow down your results — useful when you know what word you need to fill, or when a game or exercise specifies a particular type.

    Best Practices for Using Adverbs in Writing

    Adverbs are often used incorrectly and can clutter up a beautiful piece of writing. But when used well, adverbs are quite versatile. Here are 4 ways to get the most out of them.

    • Choose specific over generic — Instead of saying "she spoke in a nervous way," say "She spoke hesitantly." The manner adverb does the work of a phrase and is often more vivid than a phrase. Use the Manner filter to find these.
    • Mix up adverb types — A passage mostly comprised of manner adverbs can read as dull and become richer by introducing time adverbs (subsequently) and degree adverbs (barely). Generating from different type filters in sequence allows you to create a rich palette of options.
    • Placement matters — Adverb placement can change the meaning of a sentence. For example, "Only she knew" is quite different from "She only knew." Try placing a good adverb in three different locations in a sentence.
    • Difficulty levels — Use of words like 'quickly', 'never' and 'here' is best in dialogue. Words like 'pellucidly', 'stertorously' and 'assuredly' are best used in literary fiction or academic writing. This ensures that your prose does not become too bland or even affected.

    How to Avoid Adverb Overuse

    Most writing advice about adverbs falls into one of two categories: "Use them sparingly" and "Avoid them." But the problem with adverbs is not the adverbs themselves; the problem with adverbs is that they are used to prop up weak verbs.

    Thus, "She walked quickly" could be rewritten as "She hurried" or "She strode." In those cases, it is better to use the stronger verb and eliminate the adverb altogether. Adverbs are also necessary for modifying other words and for creating particular effects, such as in dialogue (frequency, time) ("Never," she said. "Immediately," I said). A manner adverb adds unique information to a verb (She smiled sadly. Her smile was sad), to an adjective (very bare, almost invisible, barely visible), or to another adverb (as surprisingly as possible).

    This generator is great for finding alternatives to overused adverbs in a manuscript. Run it to generate a list of 20 manner adverbs, scan through for synonyms and near-synonyms, and then use them consistently throughout your longer work. The resulting writing will read more varied and alive than if you'd simply cut all the adverbs.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the difference between an adverb and an adjective?

    Adjectives are used to modify nouns (a quick response). Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs (she responded quickly). Many adverbs are based on the same root as their corresponding adjective (quick/quickly). If a word answers the questions how?, when?, where?, or how often? then that word is an adverb.

    Can adverbs modify other adverbs?

    You can use them to a degree. Adverbs such as very, quite and barely are very commonly used in conjunction with other adverbs, e.g. She runs incredibly quickly. He arrived almost immediately. Although this is grammatically correct, it is best used sparingly, as it often indicates that a verb is being misused and should be changed instead.

    Why should I vary the adverbs I use?

    One reason is that repeating the same adverbs can dull your storytelling. Varying what adverb you use will add variety to your language and keep the reader engaged. Varying adverbs of manner, degree, and frequency keeps readers engaged and adds depth to your language.

    How does difficulty filter work?

    Easy words: These are the most common, highest-frequency adverbs. Most native speakers will recognize these immediately (quickly, never, here). Medium words: These are less common than Easy words, but most native speakers are able to understand them (tenaciously, intermittently, profoundly). Hard words: These are the least common and are often used in literary or technical writing. They are good for academic writing or vocabulary building, but are not easily recognized by most native speakers.

    What does the adverb filter type do?

    Using this filter will help narrow down the results for you. For example, by selecting Manner, you will get results such as "gracefully" and "boldly" (how). By selecting Frequency, you will get results such as "rarely" and "always" (how often). This category can be useful for a number of games, such as one that asks for a manner adverb.
