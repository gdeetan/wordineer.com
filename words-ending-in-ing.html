About words ending in -ING

The "-ing" suffix is among the most productive in English. It turns any verb into a gerund (swimming is fun), a present participle (she is swimming), or an attributive adjective (a swimming pool). Because it is so common, "-ing" words saturate everyday speech and writing — every sentence with an ongoing action uses one.

Part of speech: Most are verbs acting as gerunds or present participles. Some are established nouns (morning, ceiling) or adjectives (amazing, surprising) that no longer feel like verb forms.

Using -ING words in writing and word games

In board word games, "-ing" is valuable because you can extend many existing verb plays. Knowing which verbs take clean "-ing" forms (no doubled consonant, no dropped E) gives you a fast mental shortlist for tight spaces.
