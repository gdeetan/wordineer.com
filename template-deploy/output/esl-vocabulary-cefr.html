What is CEFR?

The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) is the international standard for describing language proficiency. It uses six levels: A1 and A2 (basic user), B1 and B2 (independent user), and C1 and C2 (proficient user). Most everyday-conversation goals sit at B2. Academic and professional contexts usually require C1.

Methodology

Data sources. The word list is the Wordineer CEFR ESL Vocabulary dataset (v1.0.0), 62 headwords across A1–C2. Frequency scores use the wordfreq Python library, Zipf scale, English. Pronunciations are transcribed from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary via NLTK.

How levels are assigned. Levels come from the source dataset. Wordineer does not assign or re-tag levels by editorial judgment. If a word is not in the dataset at a given level, it is not published at that level.

How the featured cards are chosen. Within each level, cards are the highest-frequency words by wordfreq Zipf score, up to a cap of 12 per level. Function words (articles, pronouns, auxiliaries, prepositions, conjunctions) and proper nouns are excluded from the card set. The full alphabetical table below the cards on each level page shows every word at that level.

IPA source and convention. Pronunciations are General American IPA, converted from ARPAbet using the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary. Convention: primary stress ˈ, secondary stress ˌ, no syllable dots, no length marks. R-colored schwa is written ər when unstressed and ɜr when stressed. Words with multiple CMU pronunciations use the first entry.

Definitions and examples. Written by Wordineer and reviewed by Garrick Dee. Every definition and example on this site passes through a two-column review file (draft → approved) before publication.

Known limits. The v1.0.0 dataset is deliberately small (62 headwords) to keep review quality high while the pipeline is proven. Some CMU entries were missing for rarer C-level headwords; those are flagged and published without IPA rather than with a guess.

Frequently asked questions

Which CEFR level should I study?

Pick the level one step above where reading feels easy. If B1 reading is comfortable, work on B2 vocabulary. Learning slightly above your current level builds comprehension faster than reviewing words you already know.

How does CEFR map to IELTS and TOEFL?

Under the TOEFL iBT 1–6 scale (in use for tests taken on or after January 21, 2026), a total score of 5 aligns with C1, 4 with B2, 3 with B1, 2 with A2, and 1 with A1. IELTS 7 aligns with C1, 6.5 with B2, and 5.5 with B1. See the exam mapping table above for exact bands and sources.

Where do the CEFR levels for these words come from?

Levels are read from Wordineer's cefr-lexicon dataset. Levels are not assigned by editorial judgment. If a word is not in the source dataset at a given level, it is not published at that level.

Can I print the lists or use them in class?

Yes. Each level page prints cleanly, and every level has a CSV download. Content is available under CC BY 4.0 with attribution to Wordineer.

Can I import the CSV into Anki or Quizlet?

Yes. Each CSV has one row per word with columns: word, pos, level, ipa, definition, example. Both Anki and Quizlet accept comma-separated files with a header row.

Why is a particular word at this level?

Level assignment comes from the CEFR lexicon source. Wordineer does not re-tag levels by judgment. Within a level, the featured cards are the highest-frequency words according to wordfreq Zipf scores; the alphabetical table shows the full level.
