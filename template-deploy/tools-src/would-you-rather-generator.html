    What is a Would You Rather Generator?

    Use a Would You Rather generator to create random dilemma questions to use in your conversations and discussions. Each question is set up to provide two different options, and then you choose which you would prefer to do. By forcing you to choose one option over the other, it reveals how you think, what you value, and what you are willing to live with. That is what makes it so fun in discussions with others.

    There are over 130 Would You Rather questions for your use in seven different categories: Funny, Hard, Deep, Kids and Family, Couples, Work, and Dark. These are all carefully curated for use with specific groups in varying circumstances. For example, you can be certain that a "Dark" question will not be generated for 8-year-old children, and that a "Kids and Family" question will not be generated for a meeting of adults at work. Select a category, enter the number of questions you require, and they will be generated instantly for your use within 10 seconds.

    Why Use a Would You Rather Generator?

    For those new to the game, good Would You Rather questions are harder to think of than they look. Most people can come up with 3-4 questions before they start repeating themselves, and most of them are so well known that everyone knows the answer. A generator helps create consistent new questions for each game without the stress of coming up with new ones each time.

    The game itself is more useful than it appears at first. For one, it is a unique medium in which people can reveal their true preferences, values, and even their personality in a low-risk environment. It's a unique opportunity for people to really find out things about each other. Questions that elicit a real reaction and reveal true answers include: Would you rather be rich and unknown for the rest of your life or famous your whole life and bankrupt? Would you rather always tell the truth and suffer the consequences, or never tell the truth no matter what? These are the kinds of questions that, in the course of a normal conversation with someone, would take a long time to find out.

    Would You Rather is one of the best works and professional event icebreakers because it uses only hypothetical questions. For the Work section of Would You Rather Questions, we came up with hypothetical career dilemmas that spark good discussions with colleagues or at professional networking events, and you never have to reveal anything too personal, as you might when reading a book.

    How It Works

    On the left side of the screen, you can select categories and set the number of questions for the random test. By clicking "Generate Questions," you get a random set of questions for the category selected. Each test question is presented with both options, A and B, so everyone can read them.

    The generator keeps track of which questions you have already seen for each session and won't present them to you again until you have gone through the entire set of questions for that category. The pool of questions for each category automatically resets when it has been exhausted. You can manually reset a category's repeats at any time.

    Hide cards hides all of the questions on the page. You can then have one person show the cards one at a time to the rest of the group. Use the copy button to get all of the questions as plain text. Use the Print button to get a clean two-column page with all of the cards, no ads, and no navigation.

    Categories — What's in Each One?

    Funny – These are absurd, somewhat silly questions that often have no right answer. Examples include speakers speaking only in rhyming speech and people with fingers as long as their legs and people with legs as short as their fingers. These are the safest of the games and work for almost any group.

    Hard – actual dilemmas that require a little thought to resolve. Loss of sight or hearing? True love with no money or all the money in the world with no love? Knowing the date of your own death or the cause of it? These will prompt the most discussion about any of the list but are likely to slow down a 'race' type game.

    Deep — More philosophical and introspective. Some of these questions even probe at issues of purpose, legacy, freedom, and connection. For best results, use these with smaller groups, or even as a single-player exercise, to have a meaningful conversation rather than play a game.

    Kids — Dilemmas where you can use your imagination to think about superpowers, your ideal pet, the greatest adventure ever, and all about magic. This is completely family-safe and works for kids from about age 5 up.

    Couples — Work well for date nights or even newly intimate couples trying to get a handle on the other's style & priorities. Hypothetical and all about lifestyle and priorities.

    Work — career and workplace dilemmas. These scenarios are safe for use in professional settings and are 100% hypothetical.

    Dark – Most of the difficult truths we could ever face, to lead you to places beyond just being funny. For the mature adult looking for something more with the game than the Funny questions will take you.

    How to Run a Great Round

    The mechanics of Would You Rather are pretty simple – just read out the question and have people choose between A and B. But how you handle the choices people make between A and B is what will really make or break the quality of your session.

    • Require a reason. It is usually much more interesting to hear the reasons behind people's choices than the actual choices themselves. Therefore, after everyone has chosen, go around the circle and have each person explain why they chose A or B.
    • Match the category to the players in the room. Start with 'Funny' questions when first getting to know each other, and then move to 'Hard' or 'Deep' questions as players become more comfortable. Use 'Dark' questions for small groups of people who know each other very well.
    • Use Hide Cards for surprise questions. This will allow you to reveal a card one at a time and, after each player has revealed their card, read the question aloud to the group.
    • Moving on: Keep the game moving by not allowing a question to get stale and not getting hung up on a particularly 'bad' question – just re-generate.
    • Save the good ones. Even better: Use the save button to add really great Would You Rather questions to your own personal shortlist of best prompts. Over time, you'll have developed a list of quality questions that work particularly well for you.

    Would You Rather as a Drinking Game

    This game is easily adapted into a drinking game. The simplest way to play is for everyone to reveal their answer, and for the minority to drink (in a tie, everyone drinks). A stricter version would have the person who gives the worst reasons for their choice drink. Funny and Dark answers work best for this version of the game, as Hard and Deep answers tend to slow down the pace of the game too much for a drinking game.

    Would You Rather FAQs

    How many questions should I generate per round?
    For most groups, five questions are the right amount of questions to keep the discussion going without wearing people out. For larger parties where everyone reads questions out loud one at a time, increasing to 8-10 Questions can offer even more variety. But for a couple on a date night or a long road trip, for example, generating 1-2 Questions at a time to explore in detail before generating more will typically yield far more valuable and meaningful conversations than swimming through a large batch of Questions.
    What category should I start with?
    Funny questions are good for starting off because they are easy to answer and generally not too serious for a group that does not know each other very well (ie mixed ages). Once the group feels comfortable and engaged with each other, you can switch to more challenging Hard or Deep questions. Dark questions are best saved for very small groups that are very close to each other and are willing to go through some potentially uncomfortable questions together.
    Is there a Would You Rather generator for kids?
    Yes. All scenarios in the Kids category are designed to test the child's imagination using superpowers, fantasy pets, magical objects, and other adventure themes. All the questions and scenarios in the Kids category are family-friendly and suitable for children from about 5 years old and up. The Funny category is also suitable for children; however, a few of the questions may be more suitable for adults.
    Can I use this as a work icebreaker?
    The work category is for work scenarios (career decisions and trade-offs, work preferences, etc.); all questions are strictly professional in nature. The Funny category is also suitable for teams. Using completely absurd scenarios in hypothetical questions leads to very funny discussions without the fear of embarrassment.
    What happens when the tool runs out of questions?
    The generator tracks questions that have been shown during the current session and will try to avoid repeat questions of a certain type until all questions of that type have been shown. After that, the generator will automatically reset to avoid repeated questions of the same type. You can also click "Reset Repeats" to start tracking avoids for repeated questions for the current session again. Note that this only resets the tracking of repeat questions and not the rest of the settings for the current session.
    Can I print the question cards?
    Yes. Print Cards opens the browser print dialog. Print view shows a simplified two-column layout of cards with no navigation, no controls, and no ads. These can be printed out and then cut to create a physical set of cards to use for things like outdoor events, classroom activities, and games to play at home on game nights when you don't want people to be using their phones.
    What's the difference between Hard and Dark?
    Hard questions are very difficult for us to deal with, i.e., two very reasonable ways to act, each with significant consequences, and we have to choose between them (e.g., lose your sight vs. lose your hearing; true love with no money vs. wealth with no love). Dark questions, on the other hand, generally involve issues of mortality, loss, betrayal, and other 'difficult' truths. Both are for adults, but Dark stories have a different impact and generally work better for people who know each other well.
