About words ending in -EST

The "-est" superlative suffix transforms an adjective into its highest degree. "Fast" becomes "fastest," "bright" becomes "brightest," "large" becomes "largest." For short adjectives (one or two syllables), "-est" is the standard superlative form. For longer adjectives, English prefers "most" instead — "most beautiful" not "beautifullest." Understanding this split is fundamental to correct usage.

Part of speech: Mostly superlative adjectives meaning "most" (fastest, brightest, largest). Also some basic words where "-est" is not a suffix (chest, forest, harvest, interest).

Using -EST words in writing and word games

"BEST," "REST," "TEST," and "NEST" are among the most played three-and-four-letter words in board word games. The "-est" family also includes longer plays like "PROTEST" and "CONTEST" that can open or close multiple lanes at once.
