How does Rock Paper Scissors work?

Rock Paper Scissors is a quick and easy hand game to play with someone. You and the other player both pick a rock, paper, or scissors at the same time.

Here's how it shakes out:

  • Rock smashes scissors
  • Scissors cut paper
  • Paper wraps rock
  • Same pick? Tie, go again.

In other words, there's no option that's inherently better than another. Therefore, Rock Paper Scissors is a great way to see who gets the last slice of pizza, because it's virtually impossible to intentionally out-pick the other player.

Is there a winning strategy against the computer?

The tool is set up so the computer picks Rock, Paper, or Scissors totally at random for each round of play — 1 in 3 for each. The computer does not remember previous throws by you and does not attempt to find patterns in your play. So, no matter what approach or strategy you use to beat the computer in the long run, you'll win about a third of the time.

When playing against another human being, the only things that are generally true are:

  • They repeat a move that just won
  • They avoid the move that just lost
  • Rock is the most popular opening throw

Against human opponents we can increase our chances of winning by throwing Paper a little more often. This is because in Rock-Paper-Scissors matches against humans, Rock is the most frequent opening throw, so Paper is the best response.

Uses in classrooms and decisions

You can find Rock Paper Scissors everywhere: kids play for picking first spots, adults use it to decide who has to take out the trash. Even huge amounts of real money changed hands playing Rock Paper Scissors: in 2005 two art collectors had Christie's and Sotheby's play Rock Paper Scissors to see who would get to auction off a $20 million piece of art.

Teachers can also teach probability, game theory, and biology using Rock Paper Scissors.

  • Probability — three outcomes, each with a 1-in-3 shot
  • Game theory — the smartest long-run play is to pick randomly
  • Biology — In side-blotched lizards, a rock-paper-scissors cycle determines when individuals of different colors will mate; each color type will beat one type and lose to another.

Not bad for a hand game.

Frequently asked questions

What are the rules of Rock Paper Scissors?

Rock Paper Scissors is a simple game played with hands by two people. In the game there are three options or choices and each one beats the other two and loses to one. The choices are Rock which smashes Scissors, Scissors which cuts Paper, and Paper which covers Rock. If both people playing choose the same option then the result is a tie.

What is the best strategy for Rock Paper Scissors?

Because the computer randomly selects from Rock, Paper or Scissors, no strategy can outsmart it and thus increase your winning chances greater than 33.3%. However, this is not the case when playing against human opponents who are not making their picks randomly. For such opponents, one can rely on a strategy that repeats the move that won in the previous round, does not play a move that lost in the previous round and selects Rock more often than the other two moves as the first move. To beat opponents who select Rock more often as their first move, one can play Paper more often.

What is the probability of winning Rock Paper Scissors?

By making random picks, in the long run you will have 1/3 of your picks result in a win, 1/3 of your picks result in a loss, and 1/3 of your picks result in a tie. This tool helps you to keep a running count of your picks.

How is Rock Paper Scissors used to make decisions?

The game is played all around the world to settle a disagreement or to make a decision in a very fair manner. It is used for playground fights, to flip a coin for a sports event, business dealings, and yes even to bid on artwork such as was the case for a 2005 Christie's/Sotheby's art auction. Rock Paper Scissors even has a World Championship held every year where the strategy of the game is put to test against other players in hopes of being the best.
