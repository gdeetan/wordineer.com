    Using Gen Z Slang in Writing

    The core rule: two or three terms, not twenty

    Too much slang in a character's dialogue is probably the biggest problem writers have with writing Gen Z terminology. Real people code-switch, so they don't use the same slang-filled vocabulary all the time, let alone use every piece of slang they know. This example, from a character trying to sound as Gen Z as possible, is so over the top that it immediately reads as parody rather than a real person. Restrict yourself to the two or three slang terms that feel most authentic to your character's voice, and use them as needed.

    Trend status matters more than you think

    There's a whole slew of slang terms, and they generally correlate to age and demographics within Gen Z. While it's currently acceptable to use the term "yeet" in conversation (it means to throw something with great force, typically with the intent to destroy it), this term is likely to be obscure by 2025, and by 2027, it'll be a term that your dad uses to try to be funny. The trend status is current for contemporary fiction and screen dialogue; use 'trending' and 'current' for the most up-to-date terms. Established terms are also acceptable to use for fiction set in 2022–2023. Use 'fading' terms if your character is intentionally out-of-touch.

    Context determines whether slang feels authentic

    On the page, Gen Z slang can look incredibly out of place depending on the context. For it to read naturally, the slang term must match the character's age, background, and the social context in which they are speaking.     Dialogue vs. narration

    Slang typically appears in dialogue, but when used within third-person narration (whether limited or omniscient), it can read as the author trying too hard. First-person narrators have more freedom to use slang, but they should follow the same principles as above regarding selective use as opposed to saturation.

    What does the trend status mean?

    Trending means gaining popularity. Still being used by a lot of people. Current means people still use it but past its viral stage. Established means it's been around for a while, and even older generations have heard of the term. Avoid using 'fading' terms since they're no longer used; using them may make you seem out of touch.

    Should I use Gen Z slang in my writing?

    Use it, but only when appropriate and in a way that reflects how you'd like to depict the character. Ensure that you understand the context and meaning of the term before using it. Misusing it may cause the reader to lose interest in the story or article.
