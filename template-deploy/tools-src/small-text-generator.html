How to use the small text generator

Type or paste your text into the input field — all three style cards update in real time. Small Caps replaces lowercase letters with compact Unicode capitals and stays on the baseline. Superscript raises characters above the line, like exponents. Subscript lowers them below, as in chemical formulas. Click Copy on whichever card you need. Note that superscript and subscript only cover the letters and digits the Unicode standard defines for those positions — characters without a mapped equivalent are left unchanged.

Frequently asked questions

What is a small text generator?

A small text generator converts regular text into Unicode characters that appear smaller or repositioned — small caps (ꜱᴍᴀʟʟ ᴄᴀᴘꜱ), superscript raised above the line, or subscript lowered below. These are plain Unicode code points, not CSS tricks, so they paste correctly into any modern text field.

Why do some letters stay unchanged in superscript or subscript?

The Unicode standard only includes superscript and subscript forms for the letters most useful in scientific and mathematical notation — not the full alphabet. Letters with no Unicode equivalent are left unchanged. Small caps has broader coverage because it draws from the International Phonetic Alphabet block, designed to cover the complete Latin alphabet.

Can I paste small text into Instagram or Discord?

Yes. All three styles are real Unicode characters and paste into Instagram bios, Discord usernames, Twitter/X profiles, TikTok display names, and any field that accepts plain text. The styling survives the paste because it lives in the characters themselves, not in markup.

What is the difference between superscript and small caps?

Small caps replaces lowercase letters with smaller versions of capital letters while staying on the baseline — the result looks like a typeface style. Superscript shifts characters above the baseline, as seen in footnote numbers and exponents. Subscript shifts characters below the baseline, as in chemical formulas like H₂O.

Do search engines read small text Unicode as real words?

No. Search engines and screen readers treat these Unicode characters as their own code points, not as the Latin letters they resemble. A page where "ꜱᴍᴀʟʟ" appears in the body won't rank for the word "small." Use small text for decorative or social-media copy only — not for content you need indexed or read aloud.
