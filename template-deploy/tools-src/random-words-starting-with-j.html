    J: the youngest letter in the English alphabet

    J and I were originally the same letter — J was simply a decorative flourish used at the ends of words. The split was formalized by grammarians in the 1600s, making J the newest letter in the Latin alphabet. Because of its late arrival, J borrowed heavily from other languages: junta and jalapeño came through Spanish, jubilee and jasper arrived via Hebrew through Latin, and jackal entered English from Turkish through Arabic. This foreign lineage gives J words a distinctly vivid, often exotic character compared to the older letters of English.

    J words worth using in your writing

    Jubilant is more vivid than happy or elated — it carries a sense of triumphant, overwhelming joy that simpler synonyms cannot convey. Judicious means showing careful, sound judgment, more precise than wise or smart. Jocular describes a playfully good-humored tone — more specific than funny. Juxtapose is genuinely irreplaceable: when you place two things side by side for contrast, no single English word does exactly that. Jaundiced describes a cynical or prejudiced viewpoint shaped by bitter experience. Jeopardize implies real, serious stakes in a way that risk or endanger sometimes does not. Juncture names a critical turning point — more precise than time, moment, or point.
