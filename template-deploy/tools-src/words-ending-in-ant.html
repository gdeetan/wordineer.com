About words ending in -ANT

Like "-ent," the suffix "-ant" derives from a Latin participial ending. It produces adjectives of quality (relevant, pleasant, significant) and agent nouns (accountant, tenant, participant). The two suffixes are interchangeable in origin but not in modern English — specific words lock in one or the other, and spelling both correctly requires memory rather than rules.

Part of speech: Adjectives describing an ongoing state (elegant, ignorant, abundant) and nouns for people or agents (servant, merchant, assistant). The suffix parallels "-ent" in meaning and origin.

Using -ANT words in writing and word games

Because "-ANT" and "-ENT" share the same phoneme in many accents, word-game players need to memorise which ending each word takes. Keeping a mental list of common "-ant" words (servant, elegant, pleasant) prevents costly spelling errors.
