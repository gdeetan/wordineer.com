    T's prefix family and frequency

    Trans- (across) gave transport, transform, transcend, and transparent. Tri- (three) gave triangle, trilogy, and triumph. The root temp- (time) gave temporary and contemporary. The root terr- (land/earth) gave territory and terrain. T is so common in English partly because "the" — the most frequently used word in the language — starts with it, and partly because many Old English function words (this, that, they, there, then, thus) all begin with T. Together, grammatical necessity and prefix productivity make T appear in roughly 9% of all letters in typical English text.

    T words worth using in your writing

    Taciturn means habitually silent — more precise than "quiet" or "reserved". Tenacious means holding firmly to a position or purpose — stronger than "determined". Terse means brief and abrupt in speech — different from "short" in implying a deliberate cutting of words. Transient means lasting only a short time — more precise than "temporary". Tumultuous describes a loud, confused, or excited state — more vivid than "chaotic". Torpid means mentally or physically inactive — more specific than "slow" or "lazy". Turbulent describes irregular, unsteady movement or a state of conflict — stronger than "rough" or "unstable".

    T in word games

    T is worth 1 point in Scrabble and one of the most common tiles, making it valuable for connecting words and building long plays. Short plays like tab, tan, and tar fill board spaces efficiently. Five-letter T words like table, there, thumb, and tight are solid Wordle starters — T appears frequently as both first and last letter. The combination -tion ending makes T especially useful at the end of words for scoring with long plays, and many -tion words can be extended or anagrammed for bonus points in competitive Scrabble.
