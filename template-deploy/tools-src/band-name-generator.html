    What makes a great band name?

    Of course, no single rule applies to all band names but the names that work usually have a few of the following qualities.

    Memorability

    Bands should have easy-to-remember band names. This can come from a strong sound. Some good band names contain words with clear consonants that make a 'popping' sound, or a strong rhythm. Read the name out loud and see if it sounds good in a sentence, i.e. 'Have you heard of Neon Wolves?'

    Pronounceability

    It also has to be pronounceable by strangers and not be a real pain for people to refer to you in conversations, look up your records, or search for your name on Spotify, etc. Misspelling words is clever, but only if it's also intuitive to the spelling of the word. Unusual phonetics can make for a poor band name, no matter how good you are.

    Googleability

    The band name is also a search query. Very generic names will get lost among hundreds of thousands of similar results. Very unusual names will not get any results at all. The aim is to find a name that can be optimized for Google in such a way that you end up on the first page of results for the band name, ideally with no other band listed.

    Visual identity

    The name also serves as your band's printed logo for posters, T-shirts, CD covers, etc. So, shorter names work better for printing on T-shirts, and names with a distinct shape in print create a stronger visual identity. Examine how the name looks written out in full.

    Room to grow

    Don't create a band name that locks you into your current music. When you change styles, a band name like "Death Punk Ragers" doesn't fit well with acoustic folk. Add some abstraction to your band name.

    Band name ideas by genre

    The style of band names found within genres is well known. So, when viewing band name suggestions on this site, viewers can easily judge whether a name fits a certain genre.

    Rock

    Rock band names are typically physical and evoke the open road. Think: roads, machines, weather, and geography. Examples of rock band names would be 'Broken Highway' and 'Thunder and Iron'. Something that looks a bit worn and has been scrawled on a tour van in years past would be ideal. Anything too glossy or mainstream sounding is also terrible.

    Metal

    The dark, metal name goes for mythic and is composed of words like "obsidian", "void" or "eternal". What works for a weapon or a battle standard also works for a metal band. These names are part of the metal vocabulary and therefore also obvious choices for a metal band.

    Indie

    Indie band names are often very small and highly specific to the name of an item of common, everyday wear or some item that you might find in your home or in the post. These names are typically written out in an 'underline' hand, such as for a poem title, rather than as some superhero name.

    Hip-Hop

    The Hip-hop genre is all about standing out and showing who you are. Think street cred. Wu-Tang Clan, Run DMC, or Public Enemy. Short, easy to remember. Before choosing a name, say it out loud. Does it stick? Will fans remember it? Imagine the announcer introducing your band and the crowd reaction.

    Folk

    A folk band name might reference the natural world or some aspect of the earth that is solid and rooted. Trees, streams, fire, candles, a hollow, these kinds of images are perfect for poster work hand-painted on plywood to be stuck up on barns. Avoid anything too 'made' or 'slick'.

    Jazz

    Jazz: Use words that suggest a certain feeling. We call these words suggestive. Suggested words are often indicative of the quarter in which the band plays. Midnight, smoke, alleys, and quarters can all be suggested by a band name. Use short, elegant words instead of long words that are seldom used outside of elaborate written communications.

    Tips for choosing your final band name

    The time to start researching your band name has come to a close. Follow these steps to make sure the name you choose is perfect for you.

    Type your band name into Google, with the exact words being searched (i.e., "Broken Color" in quotes). Check the top half of the first page of results. If a band with the same name as yours has dominated that space, then scrap your name, it's far too much hard work to compete with somebody else's Search Visibility for their own band name.

    Search Spotify by the name of your band. Do results appear for that band name? Even if you are a more established band than one that lists under the name you're considering, having them listed first on your band name's page will render your work invisible for most of your fans, and it will automatically be the preferred choice in all artist pages to which your band's name appears.

    Check the social media handles: Search for @[yourname] on Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook. Ideally, you'll want the same handle across all of them. If the exact name is taken but you can find a close variation (e.g., you can't get @[bandname] but @[bandnameband] is available), then it may be worth you holding onto that as well for obvious reasons.

    Check for domain name availability. Ideally, a band would want the .com version of their band name, but someone has often already snagged it. In that case, consider .band, .music, or other suitable alternatives, and make sure to see who owns the .com version, even if it's an old defunct band, to ensure that your fans don't get sent to the wrong site.

    Check if the name is trademarked. Search the USPTO database (for US music) or an equivalent in your country. If other bands use it, look for another name. You don’t want to end up being a copycat or getting sued because you used something that already has a trademark. This is a critical step, and getting it right is critical.

    Show your friends and family. Ask for their feedback. How does it sound? Is it catchy? Have them write your band name and see if they can recall it.
