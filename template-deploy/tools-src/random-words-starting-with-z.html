    Z: English's rarest letter

    Z appears in less than 0.07% of written English text, making it by far the rarest letter in the alphabet. Most Z words entered English through Arabic (zero, zenith, azure came through Arabic mathematical and astronomical vocabulary), Greek (zone, zodiac, zeal), and German (zeitgeist, zeppelin). Old English had almost no Z words — the letter was so rare that medieval scribes often used it only for borrowed vocabulary. This foreign origin gives Z words a distinctive, often exotic character.

    Z words worth knowing

    Zealous means showing great energy and enthusiasm for a cause — more intense than "enthusiastic". Zenith means the highest point reached — more precise than "peak" or "top" in implying an absolute maximum. Zeitgeist means the defining spirit or mood of a particular period in history — borrowed from German and genuinely irreplaceable, with no English equivalent. Zeal means great energy and enthusiasm for a cause — the root of zealous, useful as a noun. Zephyr means a soft, gentle breeze — more poetic and specific than "breeze". Zealot means a person who is fanatical about a cause — stronger than "enthusiast".
