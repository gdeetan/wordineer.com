About words ending in -ISH

The "-ish" suffix does several distinct jobs. As a diminishing modifier it softens an adjective: "reddish" means somewhat red, "childish" means resembling a child. As a nationality marker it identifies speakers and cultures: English, Spanish, Turkish. In informal modern usage it even modifies numerals ("I'll be there at 7-ish") — a productive informal pattern. The overlap with verb endings like "finish" and "vanish" means not every "-ish" word is an adjective.

Part of speech: Adjectives meaning "somewhat resembling" (reddish, childish, foolish), nationality adjectives (English, Spanish, Swedish), and some verbs (finish, vanish, polish).

Using -ISH words in writing and word games

In word games, "-ISH" endings help extend adjectives already on the board. "SELFISH," "STYLISH," and "OAFISH" are efficient plays that score well without requiring unusual letters.
