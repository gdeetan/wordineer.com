    S's prefix family

    Sub- (under) gave submarine, subvert, and subtle. Super- (above) gave superior, supersede, and superlative. Semi- (half) gave semicircle and semifinal. Syn- and sym- (together) gave synchronize, sympathy, and synthesis. The Latin root sens- (feel) gave sensitive, sensible, and sensation. Scrib- (write) gave scribe and describe. This makes S one of the most generative letters in English for building scientific, academic, and formal vocabulary.

    S words worth using in your writing

    Sardonic describes dry, mocking humor that is slightly cruel — more precise than "sarcastic". Scrupulous means having a careful regard for doing what is right — stronger than "careful" or "thorough". Stoic describes someone who endures hardship without complaint — from the ancient Greek school of philosophy. Sublime means of such excellence that it inspires awe — stronger than "beautiful" or "great". Succinct means expressing something clearly in few words — more precise than "short" or "brief". Sycophant names a person who flatters powerful people for personal gain — irreplaceable for that specific combination.
