    What is a random noun generator?

    This tool pulls random nouns from a curated list of words (hundreds). Unlike the Random Word Generator, which produces completely random words (parts of speech, etc), the Noun Generator produces only nouns, so it's very targeted. If you're specifically looking for nouns and don't want to bother adjusting the filter setting, this is the tool for you.

    Unlike the word generator, which has a different set of filters, the noun generator has specialized filters such as abstract, concrete, or proper nouns, depending on what you need. Each filter significantly changes the word generator's output and can therefore be very useful for each creative challenge.

    Types of nouns explained

    Users can get the most out of this tool by knowing the meaning of each type and when to use it.

    Abstract nouns

    Abstract nouns name ideas, concepts, qualities, and states that exist in the mind rather than in the physical world. Examples: freedom, grief, ambition, velocity, justice, nostalgia, courage.

    Abstract words are the language of theme and interiority. So when you are brainstorming and trying to get at what your story is really about, using the abstract filter can be very useful.

    The 10 nouns that you come up with for each concept will likely include the exact word that you've been circling as the central conflict of your story. Abstract words are also great for essays, philosophical writing, and any other writing that tries to move from the specific to the general.

    Concrete nouns

    Concrete nouns refer to physical objects that the five senses can detect (sight, hearing, touch, taste, or smell). Some examples include food, weather, a hammer, or a car. I use this filter when a scene is too vague or too abstract. The addition of two or three very specific concrete nouns to the mix immediately anchors the reader in the physical world of the piece and makes the writing real. While fiction writers use concrete nouns to make metaphors work, poets use them to create enduring images.

    Proper nouns

    Proper nouns refer to names of people, cities, companies, basically anything that's a name. Some examples include Wendy's, Seattle, the United Nations, and Beethoven. Hint: these words are always capitalized. You can use this filter for ideas for fictional names in your story.

    Common nouns

    These are categorized words that refer to people, places, or things rather than names. Some examples include: bridge, dog, cat, teacher, or work. Use the common filter for classroom vocabulary exercises, accessible writing, or when you want results that feel grounded and universal rather than exotic or specialized.

    Collective nouns

    Collective nouns refer to groups of people, animals, or things that refer to more than one noun. Examples include flock, government, colony, and herd. Unfortunately, these words are underused in writing and are something that would add color to your story or research paper.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a noun?
    A noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, or idea. Nouns come in several types: concrete nouns name physical things you can sense (river, hammer), abstract nouns name ideas and feelings (freedom, grief), proper nouns name specific individuals or places (London, Everest), common nouns are everyday general words (dog, garden), and collective nouns name groups as a unit (flock, parliament, murder).
    What's the difference between abstract and concrete nouns?
    Concrete nouns refer to things that exist in the physical world and can be perceived by the senses — hammer, cathedral, river, ember. Abstract nouns refer to concepts, emotions, or qualities that exist in the mind — freedom, grief, ambition, velocity. Both are useful for writers: concrete nouns anchor a scene in physical reality; abstract nouns convey theme and emotional depth.
    What is a collective noun?
    A collective noun names a group of people, animals, or things as a single unit. Examples: a flock of birds, a parliament of owls, a murder of crows, a pod of whales, a pride of lions. Collective nouns are more vivid and precise than generic group words like "a group of" or "a bunch of" — and they often carry a poetic quality that makes prose more memorable.

    What is a proper noun?
    A proper noun is the specific name of a particular person, place, organisation, or thing. Proper nouns are always capitalised. Examples: London, Monday, Everest, Amazon, Shakespeare. Use the Proper filter to generate proper nouns for character names, place names in fiction, or historical research.
