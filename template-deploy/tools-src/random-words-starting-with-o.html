    The ob- prefix and O's formal vocabulary

    The Latin prefix ob- means "against", "toward", or "over", and it generates a large family of formal English words: obstruct (to block against), obscure (to cover over), obstinate (standing against), oblique (slanting away), and oblivion (a state of forgetting). The Greek prefix omni- (all) gave omniscient (knowing all) and omnipotent (all-powerful). This prefix productivity makes O essential for formal, legal, and academic vocabulary.

    O words worth using in your writing

    Ominous means suggesting that something bad is about to happen — more precise than "threatening" or "dark". Opulent describes great wealth displayed lavishly — more specific than "rich" or "luxurious". Obstinate means stubbornly refusing to change — stronger than "stubborn" in formal contexts. Ostracize means to exclude someone from a group or community — no single-word synonym captures this social exclusion as precisely. Oblique means not straight or direct — useful for describing both literal angles and indirect communication. Oblivion names a state of being completely forgotten or unconscious — irreplaceable in both literal and metaphorical use.
