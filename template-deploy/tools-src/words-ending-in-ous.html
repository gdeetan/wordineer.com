About words ending in -OUS

The "-ous" suffix is heavily Latin in origin and appears across the entire vocabulary of formal English. "Obvious" (in the way), "furious" (full of fury), "gorgeous" (beautiful), "enormous" (out of the norm) — the suffix marks adjectives of quality or relationship. Because so many "-ous" words come through French, they tend to feel slightly more formal or elevated than plain Anglo-Saxon alternatives.

Part of speech: Adjectives meaning "having the quality of," "full of," or "relating to" the base. Most entered English through Latin and French (famous, glorious, nervous, tremendous).

Using -OUS words in writing and word games

In competitive word play, "-OUS" adjectives often have high-value tiles (U, O) and can reach bonus squares easily. "Luxurious," "hazardous," and "outrageous" are examples worth memorising.
