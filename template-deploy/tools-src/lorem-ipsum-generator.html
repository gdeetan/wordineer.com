Where the "Lorem ipsum" text actually comes from

De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum by Cicero. Specifically from the opening of Book I, Chapter X, section 1 (section 1.10.32 for the pedantic types). Written circa 45 BC to ponder the ethics of exceeding the natural limits imposed upon us by Nature, it's a text that has long been considered to be one of the great works of the Stoic/Positivist school of thought. It was the perfect 'thesis' to punctuate the open, educated stance of early printing.

"Neque porro quisquam est qui dolorem ipsum quia dolor sit amet, consectetur, adipisci velit…"

The Latin passage is from Cicero's De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum, written in the 1st century BC. Specifically, it is from the beginning of the first chapter of Book I, section 1.10.32, which goes on to develop the concept at greater length.

Some unknown typographer of the 16th century chose this passage to demonstrate typefaces, cutting up the words more or less haphazardly and tacking on 'Lorem' to the beginning of the word 'dolorem' (itself a halved word). Since then, the passage has been reproduced, almost without variation, for over 500 years. It is invisible because it is unreadable, so use it accordingly.

Frequently asked questions

Is Lorem Ipsum real Latin?

While the text actually is Latin (extracted from the beginning of Cicero's De Finibus), it has been jumbled, reorganized, and even augmented with made-up words like "Lorem". So, while what you read is technically Latin, it does not actually form coherent Latin sentences. A trained Latin teacher could spot an oil can full of Lieber's Latin nonsense like this in about 2 seconds.

Why start with "Lorem Ipsum dolor sit amet?"

This formatting is based on recognition. Most people would instantly recognize the first paragraph of Lorem Ipsum as filler that should be ignored. But when the toggle is turned off, the first paragraph of filler looks like a very unusual real sentence. For design reviews, you would want the toggle turned on.

When should I use Word Soup instead?

Used for Typography QA only. Latin text has an unusually smooth letter distribution. Unlike real English, which can form very wide words (e.g., responsibilities, asymmetrical) that break a narrow column, Word Soup is mixed with short and long English words to reveal any such flaws in your design.
